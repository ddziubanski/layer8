# Layer 8: Spec

## 1. Goal
A robust, self-hosted service desk that takes the best of ManageEngine (process depth), TrackIT (small-shop simplicity), KACE (endpoint and scripting muscle) and Spiceworks (easy onboarding and portal).

## 2. Must-haves
1. **Ticket intake by email.** A monitored mailbox creates tickets. Replies thread onto the existing ticket (match on ticket ID in subject or message headers). Attachments are stored with the ticket.
2. **Ticket intake by self-service portal.** Submit form (category, subject, details, attachments), "my tickets" with status, suggested knowledge base articles.
   - **Requester picks the queue.** Queues marked "show in portal" (with a friendly name and description) are offered as selection buttons, or a dropdown when there are many. The chosen queue is kept unless a routing rule explicitly matches on that chosen queue or is flagged "override the requester's choice"; other rules can still set priority.
   - **Staff use it too.** Agents and admins can open the portal (at /portal, linked from the console) to file and follow their own requests as an ordinary requester.
   - **Impact and urgency.** Requesters answer "Who is affected?" (just me / several people / immediate business impact) and "How urgent is it?" (can wait / soon / blocked now). An admin-editable matrix turns the answers into the starting priority; routing and ticket rules can still change it. Agents can reassess, which re-prioritises.
   - **Categories per queue.** Each queue has an optional category list; the form shows the chosen queue's list.
   - **SSO as the logged-on Windows user.** Opening the portal on a domain PC signs the user in silently as whoever is logged on, with no password prompt. Kerberos against on-prem AD (realm CORP.EXAMPLE), checked by the app itself with the portal SPN's keytab (no IIS or proxy); on-network/VPN only. Needs an AES service account with `HTTP/<portal host>` and the portal URL in the Local Intranet zone by GPO; users must open the portal by its DNS name. Only Kerberos is accepted (NTLM, replays and other realms are refused). The ticket names the user by sAMAccountName or, after an email-style Windows logon, by UPN; both match the synced directory user, who is brought up to date at sign-in. Set up in Settings > Windows sign-in: upload the keytab (stored in `HELPDESK_SECRETS_DIR`, mode 0600, never served back; RC4-only keytabs are refused), then turn it on and choose whether the sign-in page tries it without a click. If silent sign-in fails, the sign-in page shows a "Sign in with your Windows account" button above the password form; it isn't retried by itself in that tab, nor after signing out, nor in a browser where a technician signs in with a password. Portal only: an SSO session acts as a requester and can't reach the console; agents sign in to the console with their AD password. Browser SSO identifies the user, not the PC: device linking needs an AD last-logon or inventory lookup, or a desktop shortcut that passes the hostname.
3. **One intake path, one routing engine.** Email and portal tickets are indistinguishable once created, other than a source field. Routing sends them to any of multiple queues.
4. **Routing.** Rules on category, keywords, requester, source and priority assign a queue and priority on arrival. The matched rule is shown on the ticket.
5. **Assignment.** Queues, individual assignees, reassign and escalate, with history.
   - **Queue moves are noted.** Any agent, and always the queue's managers, can move a ticket to another queue from the ticket view. Each move adds an internal note "Moved from X to Y by Name", with an optional reason.
   - **Requester linking.** An emailed ticket is linked to the sender's account by matching their address (including AD aliases from `proxyAddresses` once directory sync exists). A tech can change a ticket's requester to the right account, found by email address or created.
6. **Attachments.** Upload from portal, email and agent UI. Files from any source (email, portal, replies) are documents on the ticket, listed in an Attachments section with who added them, when, and with which message; images, PDFs and plain text open in an in-page viewer, everything else downloads. Size limits and allowed types. Stored outside the database, referenced by ID. Malware scanning of email attachments is done by Exchange Online before delivery; the suite does not scan them. Portal and agent UI uploads are not scanned either (accepted risk).
7. **SLA timers.** Per priority, with at-risk and breached states.
8. **Notifications.** Requester gets email on updates. Replying to the email adds to the ticket.
9. **Multiple queues with owners and managers.**
   - **Owners** (KACE-style) are the agents who can be assigned the queue's tickets. Assigning to a non-owner is refused; moving a ticket to a queue where its assignee isn't an owner unassigns it. A queue with no owners yet accepts any agent, so a fresh install works. Agents get a "My queues" view.
   - **Managers** are accountable for the queue: they get escalation and SLA breach notices, and can edit the queue's settings and owners. Admins manage managers. Replies to manager notices are filed as internal notes.
   - **Restricted queues.** A queue can be restricted (e.g. Human Resources): only its owners, managers, viewers and admins see its tickets, in lists, counts, the ticket page, comments, files and history. Viewers are users or flat groups chosen in the app (no AD nesting needed). Anyone can still hand a ticket to it. A technician filing into a restricted queue is just the requester there.
   - **Users and groups.** Owners and managers can each be any mix of individual users and groups. Groups are local (members managed in the app) or directory (an AD security group or Entra group, members filled by directory sync). Which kinds are used depends on the install: standalone installs use local users and groups; AD/Entra installs can use security groups and directory users.
10. **Full backup and restore.** One command and a schedule produce a single encrypted archive (passphrase-based; the key is kept separately) of the database, attachments and raw emails, and app config. Secrets are included only inside the encrypted archive. Target: a network share (SMB or NFS, mounted by the installer). Retention policy, a verify/test-restore command, and a restore command for a fresh install. Built: Settings > Backup (folder, daily time, how many to keep, passphrase; list, back up now, verify), a worker job, and `python -m app.cli backup create|list|verify|restore`. AES-256-GCM in chunks with a scrypt key; each backup is verified before it gets its final name; restore checks everything first, keeps the old folders and upgrades the schema.
11. **Export and import between installs.** Move everything (configuration and data: users, queues, owners, rules, SLAs, tickets, comments, attachments, history) from one install of this app to another, e.g. new server or test copy. App-specific versioned format; import into an empty install, with a dry run. Built: `python -m app.cli export FILE` / `import FILE [--dry-run]`. Every table (except sessions, heartbeats, worker state, system log) as CSV through COPY, plus the file store, encrypted like backups; no secret files. Import replaces everything on an install without tickets, in one transaction, loading the columns both versions share (newer columns take defaults); still-queued email is marked not sent.
12. **Custom ticket rules (KACE-style).** Admin-defined rules beyond routing, modelled on KACE SMA custom ticket rules.
   - **Triggers:** on ticket create (after routing), on ticket update (with "what changed" conditions), or on a schedule (every N minutes, hourly, daily).
   - **Select:** a condition builder (queue, status, priority, category, source, assignee, requester/domain, keywords, age, time since update, time in status, SLA state), or full SQL like KACE.
   - **Actions:** set fields (status, priority, queue, assignee, category), add an internal note or public reply, email the requester / assignee / queue owners / managers / an address with `$ticket` template variables, escalate; or a full SQL update like KACE.
   - **SQL guardrails:** admins only. Rule SQL runs on its own database login (`HELPDESK_RULES_DATABASE_URL`) with read access to ticket data only (no credentials, sessions or settings) and update rights on a few ticket columns; it can't switch back to the app's role. A SQL update never commits: it runs in a transaction that is always rolled back, the app reads which ticket rows changed and how, checks the per-run row cap, and applies the changes itself, so history ("by rule X"), SLA timers, move notes and notifications behave exactly as for a person.
   - **No loops:** changes made by rules don't trigger on-update rules; a scheduled rule fires once per ticket until its conditions stop matching.
   - **Test and troubleshoot:** a test run executes select and update in a transaction that is always rolled back and shows matched tickets and the exact changes; each real run is logged (matches, changes, emails, errors). Rules can be limited to queues and ordered.

## 3. Screens (see GUI sample)
- Agent queue: scope (My tickets / My groups, i.e. queues I own directly or through a group / All tickets, remembered per browser) combined with saved views (All open, Unassigned, SLA at risk, Waiting on user, From email, From portal). Only the ticket list scrolls (heading, scope and views stay; column headings stick). Select tickets to move them to another queue and/or assign them together (each change noted on its ticket; per-ticket failures reported). Colour coding: each technician picks a colour (My preferences), shown as a stripe on tickets assigned to them in everyone's queue; personal colour rules (priority, status, SLA, assignee, source, queue, requester; first match wins) tint rows in their own view only. Moving one ticket: the Queue dropdown on the ticket, with the move noted.
- Ticket detail: conversation, attachments, auto-diagnosis panel, assignment, linked assets, one-click actions, reply or internal note.
- Self-service portal.
- Personal navigation (issue #36): My preferences offers a minimal sidebar preset, individual optional-module checkboxes and Show all modules. Tickets, My preferences and sign-out cannot be hidden. The preference follows the technician account across browsers and never changes permissions, direct routes, Ctrl+K destinations, background jobs or global module configuration. Defaults remain the full sidebar. Stored per user in the existing app_settings table under personal_navigation:<user id>; no schema migration.
- Settings: all configuration and setup in one area with a tab per topic (General, Queues, Ticket rules, Users, Groups, Directory, Email, Windows sign-in, Log; more to come, e.g. priority matrix, SLA, branding). The Status tab shows each part at a glance (database, worker heartbeat, directory sync, email, Windows sign-in, disk space), the server processes, and the worker's jobs (email, directory sync, scheduled rules, housekeeping) with last run, result and next run; admins can run a job now, pause it, or restart the worker (gracefully, or force-restart through a server-configured command such as systemctl). The Log tab shows the system log (directory, email, sign-in, settings changes by name only, errors with details), kept 90 days. Tabs show by role; admin-only areas are hidden from agents. Every stored setting is editable here, including credentials (kept as secret files, never shown back); a server environment variable overrides and locks a field.

## 4. Should-haves
- **Asset and user linking.** Tickets link to users and devices. Built so far: devices from AD computer objects (directory sync: name, DNS name, OS, description, enabled, lastLogonTimestamp, managedBy as the assigned person; gone from AD = inactive) and devices added by hand; a Devices page; linking devices on a ticket (the requester's own in one click), logged in the ticket history; devices readable by SQL reports and rules. Tickets arrive pre-enriched (device, last logon, switch port, VLAN, link errors) where data is available.
- **Script library.** Built so far: a Scripts page; queries (read-only SQL on the rules login with typed parameters and the period; six examples; device_id/user_id results ready for dynamic groups) and actions (PowerShell, bash, Python, Arista EOS; stored, not yet run); versions in the database with author, note, diff and restore; per-script "agents can run it" and high risk. Runners (built): Settings > Runners (token per runner, check-in status); runner/helpdesk_runner.py (stdlib only; PowerShell locally or over WinRM, bash, Python, Arista eAPI; streamed output, 10-minute limit); runs on picked devices, a device-group query or the runner itself, with a target preview, approval by another admin for high-risk scripts, cancel, and notes on the ticket they came from (docs/runner.md). From a ticket (built): a Scripts section offers the action scripts the technician may run, targets the ticket's linked devices by default, previews, runs, and lists the ticket's runs with live output; each run and its result is noted on the ticket and in its history. Schedules (built 2026-10-20): Scripts > a script > Schedules (/api/script-schedules; worker job "script_schedules"): an action on a timetable (once, daily, weekly on chosen days, monthly on a day; at a local time, as report schedules) on picked devices, a device-group query worked out at each run, or the runner itself; or a query whose result is emailed as CSV (with a period for :start/:end). It runs as its owner with the owner's rights; high-risk scripts still wait for another admin's approval each time; runs carry the schedule (script_runs.schedule_id); a run that can't happen (owner gone, group broken) is recorded on the schedule and the timetable carries on. Owners and admins edit, pause, run now, delete.
  - Read-only queries: saved reports and dynamic groups, written for Postgres. Examples to port: tickets untouched for 4 hours by owner, average days to close by category, devices not checked in for 7 or 30 days, low disk, missing purchase date, no assigned user, assigned to a non-active employee, duplicate MACs, warranty and replacement dates, users created this week, AD description changes.
  - Action scripts: PowerShell, bash, Python and Arista eAPI/CLI. Typed parameters, dry-run with target preview, approval for risky scripts, role-based access, output attached to the ticket, scheduling for recurring checks (built), versions in the database (decided; not git).
- **Quick action: the requester's AD groups and OU** (requested 2026-10-19). On a ticket, a technician can add or remove the ticket requester's security-group memberships and move the requester's account to another OU, without opening ADUC. Groups are found by search-as-you-type (name or description); OUs from a browsable/searchable list. Every change is logged on the ticket (and the system log) with before/after. To decide when building it: it writes to AD, so it needs a write-capable service account (separate from the read-only sync account) or goes through a runner's PowerShell (ActiveDirectory module) as an action script; which groups/OUs technicians may touch (an allow-list or AD delegation); whether some changes need approval like high-risk scripts. Built: the **AD account** card on the ticket runs the built-in action script "Layer 8: change an AD account" on a PowerShell runner (so an admin can mark it high risk to require approval). Allow-lists live in Settings > Directory (groups technicians may change; OUs accounts may be moved into). A preview comes first. The change is noted on the ticket and its output shows under Scripts.
- **Onboarding templates** (requested 2026-10-19; builds on the quick action above). Configured in Settings > Onboarding templates (admins; also through the API, and importable non-interactively with the installer answers file like other settings): a named template (e.g. "Finance – AP clerk") lists the security groups (search-as-you-type), the target OU and optional attributes (department, title, manager). Technicians only pick and apply templates on a ticket; they don't define them. On a ticket, applying a template to the ticket's requester (e.g. an onboarding request filed for or by the new starter) shows a preview: groups to add, groups the requester is already in, the OU move; then applies it and logs everything on the ticket. Also "copy groups from another person" as a starting point for a template. Hybrid warning: most sites sync to Entra ID, and Entra doesn't follow nested groups for many uses (group-based licensing, app assignment, Conditional Access/group claims). When a template group is nested (it has groups as members, or is itself a member of another group), the template editor and the preview flag it: "Nested group: Entra ID won't apply this through nesting (licensing, app assignment, Conditional Access). Add people directly." The sync already reads nesting, so this check needs no new AD access. Ties in with the onboarding/offboarding checklists under Later. Built: Settings > Onboarding templates (groups with nesting warnings, OU, department, title), /api/onboarding-templates, installer key `onboarding_templates`, applied from the AD account card. New accounts (built 2026-10-20): POST /api/tickets/{id}/ad-account/new[/preview] runs the built-in "Layer 8: create an AD account" on a PowerShell runner. The account name comes from Settings > Directory's format ({f}{last} -> jsmith, then jsmith2 if taken) and the UPN from the domain. The technician types the email address (suggested, checked for clashes). Fields: phone number, extension (ipPhone), mobile, manager (AD's directReports / "reports to" follows from it), department, title, office, company, description, template and extra groups (e.g. the 365 licensing group). The first password is generated, shown once to the technician and kept nowhere: it reaches the runner sealed (app/runseal.py, key in the secrets folder, cleared on claim; runner 1.1 needed). Settings > Directory "Group lists to tick from" (e.g. "Distribution lists: [EMAIL]") offers every matching group (CN or displayName) as tick boxes: GET /api/ad/group-lists. Who may create accounts (decided 2026-10-20; built): Settings > Directory "Who may create accounts": technicians and admins (default), admins only, or admins and technicians in a named group (local or synced from AD). Others don't see New account, and the API answers 403 with the reason. UI: "New account…" on the ticket's AD account card (shown even when the requester is a local account, e.g. HR filing for a starter; manager defaults to an AD requester), the password shown once with a copy button; group lists as tick boxes on the new-account form, the existing-account card (unticking a list they're in removes them) and the template editor. "Copy groups from another person" (built): on the AD account card and the new-account form, pick a colleague; their groups that technicians may change are added (nesting flagged as usual), others are counted, not added (GET /api/ad/users/{id}/groups). Start date and manager email (built): a future start date creates the account disabled and queues "Layer 8: enable an AD account" to run from the start of that day (runs have a not-before time; "Waits until …" on the ticket); "Email the manager" sends the manager the account name, sign-in and email once the account is created (never the password; replies thread into the ticket). Not yet: mailbox steps.
- **Remote session button.** Launches existing tooling and logs who, when, which device and how long onto the ticket. Options on Windows: Remote Assistance (`msra /offerra <host>`), RDP shadowing (`mstsc /shadow`), Quick Assist for off-network users. Implemented through a custom URL handler on tech workstations or a downloaded `.rdp` file. Confirm the tool is available on the target Windows builds. Built: Remote Assistance (msra /offerra) as a quick action on the ticket's **Requester's computer** field, through a helpdesk-remote: launcher on technicians' PCs (tools/remote-assistance, docs/remote-assistance.md) that reports the end with a one-time token; who, when, which computer and how long go on the ticket. The computer is filled in when the requester has exactly one assigned device, picked by the technician otherwise, and asked for in the portal ("Which computer?"); scripts on the ticket target it by default.
- **Patch visibility.** Read-only integration with Action1: compliance and pending reboots on the asset page, failed or overdue patches open tickets, patch rings link to change records. Verified 2026-10-20 against Action1's public API docs (REST 3.0: POST /oauth2/token with the API credentials; GET /endpoints/managed/{org}?fields=* gives missing_critical_updates, missing_other_updates, reboot_required, last_seen...; 50 per page; under 30 requests a minute). PSAction1 is MIT but its repository's newer Terms of Use are restrictive, so nothing is copied from it. Built (2026-10-20): Settings > Action1 (region, client ID, secret, organization, read interval, test connection, machines without a device), worker job "action1" matching machines to devices by name, patch state on the device page and the ticket's computer and device cards, and optional tickets when critical updates are missing or a reboot is pending for N days (one open ticket per machine, through the routing rules). docs/action1.md. Patch automations as changes (2026-10-20): each enabled Action1 automation that deploys updates, runs an update ring or reboots becomes a pre-approved standard change when its next run is within N days (setting, default 7), with the machines it targets as devices; it follows Action1 (moved, cancelled when disabled) and ends with the run's status (Success/Warning complete, Error fails); `changes.source` = `action1:<automation id>`.
- **Change management.** Risk-scored changes, pre-approved templates, approvals for high risk, rollback plan required. Built (2026-10-20): Changes page (list, CHG-labelled detail): kind (standard from a pre-approved template, normal, emergency), risk = impact x likelihood (low/medium/high), implementation, rollback (required to submit or start) and test plans, a window, owner, linked tickets, devices and systems, history. Draft -> awaiting approval -> approved/rejected -> in progress -> completed/failed/rolled back (or cancelled; rejected/cancelled reopen as draft). Normal changes need approval from Settings > Changes' risk level (default medium) by an approver (admins, or a named group) who isn't the owner; standard ones are approved on submit; emergency ones may start at once and are approved afterwards. Editing one awaiting approval sends it back to draft; approved ones can't be edited. Linked tickets get a note when a change starts and ends; a ticket's Changes panel lists them and raises one for it. Templates in Settings > Changes (installer key `change_templates`). A calendar (Changes > Calendar, a month by start day, coloured by risk) and overlap warnings on a change (other open changes in an overlapping window, and the devices or systems they share). Action1 patch automations arrive as standard changes on their own (see Patch visibility).
- **Knowledge base.** Draft article from a closed ticket for quick review, similar-ticket suggestions. Built (2026-10-20): Knowledge base page (/kb; search, drafts, published, archived; editor in simple Markdown with a preview, rendered without HTML; category, other words people use, who can read it: everyone in the portal or technicians only; views and "did this help?" counts; admins delete, others archive). Postgres full-text search (title and keywords weigh most; words match as prefixes so suggestions follow typing; a matching category lifts an article). Portal: Help articles (search, read, "did this help?") and "These might help" under the request's subject. Ticket: a Knowledge base panel with matching articles ("Used it" links one, logged in the history; "Copy link for the requester"), similar solved tickets in queues the technician can see, and "Draft an article from this ticket" once it's solved (problem, the last reply as the fix, internal notes to tidy; a staff-only draft). API: /api/kb/articles, /api/tickets/{id}/kb. Images (built): "Add an image…" in the editor uploads a PNG, JPEG, GIF or WebP (checked by its first bytes; no SVG) and puts its Markdown at the cursor; served from /api/kb/files/{id} to whoever may read the article, with nosniff and a no-script CSP; only those images render. Versions (built): every save that changes the title, text or keywords keeps a numbered version (author, time; drafts from a ticket say so); History on the article shows each with a diff and brings one back as a new version (published articles need the publish permission).
- **Satisfaction surveys** (requested 2026-10-20; built). Settings > Surveys: the percentage of solved tickets surveyed (picked at random when a ticket is first resolved or closed; 0 = off, the default), ask the same person at most once every N days (default 30), how long links work (default 14 days), and the question. The requester gets one email with a link per rating (1 Very unhappy to 5 Very happy); one click answers, a comment can follow; the link's token is the key (only its hash is kept), so no sign-in is needed. They can also answer on the ticket in the portal and change their answer while the link works. One survey per ticket; child tasks and merged tickets are never surveyed. Sending and answers are in the ticket history (survey_sent, survey_answered). Results (Settings > Surveys, GET /api/surveys): sent, answered, response rate, average, answers by rating, latest answers with comments and who handled the ticket. Reports (built): a "Satisfaction surveys" dataset (by technician, queue, category, rating, week/month; sent, answered, response %, average, happy %, unhappy) with ready-made reports Satisfaction by technician / queue / month. Low ratings (built): an answer at or below Settings > Surveys "Follow up answers at or below" (default 2; 0 off) adds an internal note and emails the technician who handled it and the queue's managers, once per survey.

- **Reporting.** A Reports area for agents and admins (first slice built: report engine over a tickets dataset, the ready-made reports below, period and date-field choice, table with bars, CSV export; agents see only their queues). Ready-made reports: total tickets (by period, status, source, queue, category, priority), tickets assigned per technician (open, closed, average time to close, SLA breaches), tickets opened per requester and department, SLA compliance, backlog and ageing. Plus a report builder so any data in the database can be reported on: pick what to count (tickets, users, devices...), filters, group-by and date range, shown as a table or chart and exported to CSV. Saved reports can be shared. Every report can be exported to PDF and CSV and emailed straight from the report, and scheduled: pick recipients, date, time and recurrence (daily, weekly on chosen days, monthly), sent as PDF/CSV through the service desk mailbox. Admins can also write SQL reports (built: "Report on: SQL query"; one SELECT in a read-only transaction on the restricted rules login, under the SQL rule time limit; `:start`/`:end` carry the chosen period so saved, emailed and scheduled SQL reports follow it; hidden from agents). Ties in with the read-only queries in the script library.
- **Ticket fields.** Admins edit dropdown lists on Settings > Ticket fields: resolution types and closure reasons (asked when an agent resolves or closes; each can be required), and departments (imported from AD and added automatically as new AD departments appear). Each ticket has a requester (picked from a search-as-you-type list), contact phone and department, which start from the requester's AD record and can be changed; changes are in the ticket history. Retired choices stay on old tickets and in reports. A requester closing their own ticket in the portal is recorded as "Resolved by requester". Built; later: admin-defined custom fields of any type.
- **Service notices.** Built: admins (later: a permission) post a notice of a current outage or known problem on Settings > Notices, with severity (outage / warning / information), audience (everyone / staff / requesters), start and optional end. Shown once as a dialog right after sign-in and as a banner at the top of the console and portal (including the new-request form) until it ends or is taken down; people can hide the banner for their session.
- **Branding and customization.** Built (Settings > Branding): product name, sign-in message, portal welcome text, email footer (requester emails and emailed reports), background image (sign-in page, portal, optionally console), logo and favicon (raster only, plus ICO for the favicon; SVG can carry script). Colours: a main colour and a sidebar colour, each checked for WCAG AA contrast (4.5:1) under white text, with the rest derived (hover shade, dark-mode link colour). Dark mode: off, follow each person's device, or always. A live preview shows the console in light and dark before saving.

- **Child tickets** (requested 2026-10-19). From a ticket, create a child ticket: its own subject, queue, assignee (another technician) and priority, the same requester by default; or make an existing ticket a child of another. The parent lists its children with status and assignee; a child shows which ticket it's part of. When a child is resolved the parent gets a note. A parent can't be resolved or closed while it has open children (a setting in Settings > Ticket fields). No loops: a ticket can't become a child of its own child.
- **Merging tickets** (requested 2026-10-19). Merge a ticket into another with a required reason from a drop-down (Settings > Ticket fields list, e.g. Duplicate, Same issue from someone else, Follow-up to an existing ticket, Opened by mistake). The merged ticket's replies and attachments move to the target, which gets a note naming the merged ticket and the reason; the merged ticket is closed as "Merged into #N" and keeps its history. Email replies to the merged ticket thread onto the target. The merged ticket's requester, if different, is noted on the target.
- **Ticket numbering** (requested 2026-10-19). Set at install (and in Settings > General before tickets exist): the first ticket number (e.g. 10000) and the label format shown everywhere and used in email subjects, e.g. `#{n}` (default), `HD-{n}`, `INC{n:06}`, `{yyyy}-{n}`. The number itself stays a plain, unique, increasing integer; the label is display only, and email threading recognises the configured label as well as the plain [#n] tag. Changing the format later keeps old emails threading. Built: Settings > General (first ticket number, label format with live preview), installer-importable; labels shown across console and portal and in email subjects.
- **Tickets Anywhere: work tickets by email** (requested 2026-10-19; after Spiceworks' "Tickets Anywhere", https://www.spiceworks.com/support/help-desk-cloud-edition/docs/customize-and-work/#tickets-anywhere; check its current command list when building). Technicians reply to a ticket's notification email, or email the service desk mailbox, with commands in the body that act on the ticket as if done in the console, e.g. `#close`, `#reopen`, `#assign dcole` / `#assign me`, `#priority high`, `#status waiting`, `#queue Network`, `#category Printers`, `#internal` (keep the reply as an internal note), `#time 15m`; a new email from a technician with `#create` (and optionally `#requester someone@...`) opens a ticket on someone's behalf. Only from known staff addresses (the sender is matched to an agent, as for replies); unknown commands are reported back by email instead of silently ignored; every change is in the history with "by email". Ties in with the existing intake/threading. Built: #close/#resolve [type], #reopen, #status, #assign me|name|email, #unassign, #priority, #queue, #category, #internal; #create + #requester on new email; a "By email: ..." note on the ticket; what can't be done is emailed back; a Settings > Email switch; docs/email-setup.md. Not built: #time (no time tracking yet).

## 6. Proposed architecture
- **Standard ports only.** Users and agents reach the suite on 443 (HTTPS); port 80 only redirects to HTTPS. nginx terminates TLS on 80/443 and proxies to the app, which listens on localhost only. No other ports are exposed.
- FastAPI backend, PostgreSQL, React front end.
- Background worker for mailbox polling, SLA timers, scheduled scripts and notifications.
- Object storage (local disk first) for attachments.
- Everything exposed through the API; webhooks for integrations.

### Meraki client observations (issue #38)

Optional read-only Dashboard discovery, configured in Settings > Meraki or installer section `meraki`. A dedicated read-only API key and explicit network IDs are required; an empty selection reads nothing. The worker reads a complete 24-hour client snapshot and replaces its separate observation cache atomically. Hostnames and IP addresses alone never establish identity. Ambiguous MACs do not enrich records; observed IPs and stale timestamps stay visible without editing documented IPAM fields. No Meraki configuration writes or automatic ticket creation. OAuth, diagnostic actions and webhook alerts are future slices.
